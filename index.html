SIMPLE HABITS, REAL IMPACT

Four ways to waste less

01▤

Plan before you shop

Unplanned shopping often means forgotten ingredients and food that spoils before you get to it. A quick check before you shop helps you buy what your household can actually use.

  • Check your fridge, freezer, and pantry; note what needs using soon.
  • Plan a few meals, then make a list with realistic amounts.
  • Choose loose produce when possible, and skip bulk deals unless you can use or share the extra.

Try this: Before shopping, choose one ingredient you already have and plan a meal around it.

02◷

Store food thoughtfully

Food kept at the wrong temperature or left open can spoil sooner. Good storage protects freshness and makes it easier to remember what you already have.

  • Follow package storage directions; refrigerate or freeze perishables promptly.
  • Keep opened food covered or in clean, sealed containers, and label leftovers with the date.
  • Place older items where you can see them and use them before newer purchases.

Remember: “Use by” and “best before” can mean different things. Follow local label guidance, especially for food-safety dates.

03↻

Give leftovers a second life

Extra portions do not have to become waste. Saving and planning leftovers can stretch your meals and reduce the amount of food that ends up in the bin.

  • Serve smaller portions first; people can always come back for more.
  • Refrigerate leftovers promptly in clean, shallow containers and eat them soon.
  • Freeze portions you will not use in time, label them, and reheat them thoroughly before eating.

Get creative: Add suitable leftover vegetables to soup, rice, pasta, or another meal.

04♡

Share what you can’t use

If you have more safe, good-quality food than you need, sharing it can help a neighbor or community group while preventing avoidable waste.

  • Offer food early, while it is still fresh and there is time to arrange collection.
  • Keep it covered and at a safe temperature; include ingredients or allergen information when known.
  • Only share food that has been handled safely. If you are unsure it is safe, do not donate it.

Take action: Post a suitable surplus donation here so a local volunteer can arrange pickup.
